Our vision

Energy is not something you generate. It is already there. You just need to stop giving it back.

Clean energy is usually framed as a supply problem: build more. We see an abundance problem. Useful motion is thrown away as heat every second, in places engineering has learned not to look. Our mission is to find those places and build simple, rugged machines that keep the energy, in service of a world that needs every clean watt it can get.

1The problem: energy lost where springs oscillate

A spring is a perfect reservoir with a leak at both ends. It takes energy in, then hands it right back, and the damper next to it burns the remainder as heat.

Vehicle suspensions are the largest example. Every bump, ripple, and engine or tire vibration pumps energy into the coil springs of more than a billion vehicles, and none of it is kept. The same story repeats in rail bogies, truck cabs and seats, robot joints, ship mounts, industrial machinery, and seismic isolators.

The measurement gap. Decades of “regenerative suspension” research measured the damper and reported small numbers. The spring stroke, which carries the bulk of the stored energy, was never the target. That blind spot is the opportunity.

2Why hasn't it been done?

Well-funded teams have tried to pull power from suspensions. Every one of them either targeted the damper or used powered actuators that consume energy.

Bose (2004–2017)Electromagnetic active suspension; drew 1–4 kW per corner. Never shipped; technology sold to ClearMotion.
Audi eROT (2016)Rotary damper harvester. Measured 100–150 W average, 3 W on smooth road. Never shipped.
ZF / Levant GenShockHydraulic damper harvester. Pivoted away from harvesting toward ride comfort.
ClearMotion (shipping)$365M raised, in the Nio ET9 today. Proves the market exists, but it consumes power to smooth the ride.

Our difference: we tap the spring stroke, not the damper, with a purely mechanical one-way drive. No actuators, no control power. And because the Gate pulls energy out of the suspension's motion, harvesting and ride quality move in the same direction rather than trading off.

3Our first solution: the Armstrong Gate

A gate lets traffic through in one direction. The Armstrong Gate lets spring motion through in one direction, as rotation, into a generator.

The patented drive. Both halves of the stroke turn the generator shaft the same way.

How it works

  1. 1

    A soft spring goes first. A frame biasing spring, softer than the main suspension spring, sits in series with it. Because it is the softest element, it takes the first inches of every stroke and routes that motion into the drive before the main spring deflects.

  2. 2

    Stroke becomes rotation. The drive shaft plunges through two opposed one-way (sprag) clutches with opposite-hand screw engagement. Compression grips the first clutch; rebound grips the second.

  3. 3

    Both directions spin one way. Each clutch drives the hollow generator shaft in the same direction. Up and down motion becomes continuous rotation: mechanical full-wave rectification.

  4. 4

    Fly weights smooth it, the generator keeps it. The flywheel carries rotation through every stroke reversal and the generator turns smooth rotation into electricity for the vehicle's battery. No actuators, no control power.
