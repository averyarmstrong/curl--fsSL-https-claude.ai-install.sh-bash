Behaviour the specification states (to be reproduced, not assumed):

1. "In operation, both frame biasing spring 115 and the vehicle suspension
   spring collapse simultaneously. Here, frame biasing spring 115 compresses
   more inches than the vehicle suspension spring by ratio of their strength
   coefficients until frame biasing spring 115 is completely compressed."
   (col. 6 l. 33–41) → series stack with a coil-bind hard stop.
2. "Most of the initial 2″ to 5″ of suspension compression during operation
   is received into frame biasing spring 115, and very little is received
   into the replacement suspension spring." (col. 6 l. 65 – col. 7 l. 3)

   original spring height (static ride height unchanged).
4. The generator is turned "before the energy generated by the vertical
   movement of the vehicle gets dissipated" (col. 3 l. 3–7) and the system
   "powers the generator without placing a drag on the vehicle" (col. 8
   l. 55–58). **Both are hypotheses in this repo (Q2, Q3), not inputs.**

**What it leaves open.** No numbers at all: no spring rates, no ratio, no
stroke-to-rotation transmission (the "spiral gear" is unspecified — the
ratio `r` [rad/m] is therefore a sweep parameter), no flywheel inertia, no
generator type or power, no efficiency, no vehicle mass. The only
quantitative content is the 2–5 in soft-stage stroke. Everything numerical
in later AKS documents is derived or asserted, never patented.

**Prior art cited on its face.** US 3,507,580; 3,799,481; 3,921,746;
3,980,152; 4,018,052; 4,098,083; 4,098,144; 4,223,532; 4,295,538;

## 3. `AKS_First_Principles_Foundation.md` (June 2026)

**Establishes.** The clearest earlier physics statement. Gets right:
(i) mechanical rectifier ↔ diode bridge analogy, with flywheel ↔ smoothing
capacitor; (ii) harvest from *vertical* motion imposes no longitudinal
propulsion drag to first order; (iii) a lone coil spring is conservative
(zero loop area), rectification creates loop area, and that area is the
energy per cycle; (iv) passenger-car absolute numbers are modest; the kW
story is heavy vehicles on rough duty; (v) PM generator over claw-pole
alternator; (vi) sprag lift-off 800–1500 rpm and sprag fatigue as the #1
durability risk; (vii) `P = κ·W·v` shorthand with κ≈0.10 calibrated to Audi
eROT (125 W, 1800 kg, v≈0.08 m/s).

**Gets wrong or leaves unproven (and this repo must settle).**

- "The true ceiling is the total vertical mechanical energy injected into the
  suspension … plausibly several times the cited damper figures." This is
  the central open question (Q1). The document itself labels it "an
  empirical hypothesis to measure, not a derived certainty." We derive it in
  `01_physics.md` §7 and test it numerically: for any corner under an ISO
  8608 road (white ground-velocity spectrum), the *mean* road input power is
  exactly ¼·k_t·W_v — set by tire stiffness, roughness and speed alone — and
  independent of everything above the tire, Gate included (01_physics §7). The
  series Gate redistributes that power; it does not enlarge the pie.
- `P = κ·W·v` is dimensionally a force × velocity and encodes the (false in
  general) assumption that harvested power scales with static corner load.
  Corner load does not enter the road-power formula at all; vehicle weight
  acts only through the stiffer tires heavy vehicles run. We keep κ only as a
  reporting shorthand and back it out from simulations (V4 in the old plan);
  we do not use it as a predictor.
- Vehicle table (compact EV 110 W … tank 4.7 kW average) is linear
  extrapolation from one eROT point and is tagged `asserted` in the claims
  audit.

## 4. `AKS — Diligence Response for Mohan` (Aug 2026, draft)

**Establishes — the only prototype hardware numbers that exist.**
Ebsco Spring Co. Rev 4 (2026-07-30) bench articles, carried into
`data/prototype/prototype_v1.yaml`:

| | Biasing spring EB-7234 | Main spring EB-7233 |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | 1.905 lbf/in (333.6 N/m) | 15.902 lbf/in (2785 N/m) |
| Material / wire | 316 SS, 0.187 in | 316 SS, 0.250 in |
| Free length / travel to solid | 5.50 in / 3.256 in | 7.00 in / 5.50 in |
| Load at solid | 6.2 lbf | 87.5 lbf |
| Stored energy at solid | 1.14 J | 27.2 J |

Rate ratio 8.35:1. Bench prediction stated there: preload 2.0 in, drive
±0.5–1.0 in at 2–3 Hz → 1.7–5.2 W mechanical, 1.2–3.6 W electrical. This is
a falsifiable prediction we re-derive in `05_bench_protocol.md` with our own
model and report any disagreement.

**Claims to audit.**

- "The soft biasing stage absorbs 89.3% of the elastic energy exchange
  (k_main/(k_main+k_bias))." The static series partition is textbook and
  correct *for stored energy at common force*. It is **not** a harvest
  fraction: stored elastic energy is returned every cycle unless something
  dissipates it, and the fraction *harvested* depends on the generator load,
  the clutch kinematics and the dynamics — this is φ_s (Q1), computed, never
  read off a stiffness ratio.
- "Energy exchanged per stroke ≈ corner force × stroke length … ~97–195 W
  mechanical per corner." Multiplies static preload by stroke; static
  preload does no net work over a cycle. Tagged `asserted`.
- "297.5 hp ≈ 221.9 kW per corner of transmission capacity." Treats a
  625 lb static load as 625 lb·ft of torque at 2500 rpm. The document itself
  says it is a capacity figure; it has no physical meaning as written and
  must not be repeated. Tagged `unsupported`.
- Target 30–100 W/corner (ISO C, 45–65 mph), 70–175 W (ISO D), 4–20 W
  (A/B), 600–1000 W sustained peak rating. These are `target`; experiment
  001–003 report what the model says.
- End-to-end efficiency 65–80% target with a PM generator; 41–54% with a
  claw-pole. Stage table is plausible; the generator map is `TODO(bench)`
  (PHINIA data not yet received).

## 5. `AKS_Pitch_Deck` (Google Slides, July 2026) and `AKS Deck.pptx` (Sept 2026)

**Establishes.** The public narrative. Quantitative claims to audit
(`07_claims_audit.md`):

- "19–58 W of damper dissipation on passenger cars … the spring in front of
  that damper handles a several-times-larger flow of energy every stroke."
  Reactive (stored/returned) power is being compared with real (dissipated)
  power. The *net* flow available to any harvester is the real power; see
  §3 above and `08_findings_that_change_the_story.md`.
- "Full-size pickup ~350 W average roads; loaded Class-8 ~5 kW average,
  10–16 kW rough-road peaks" via P = κ·W·v. `asserted`; replaced by
  experiment 002/006 ranges.
- "Interactive model: 287 W shown on rough-road input, single bench unit."
  The browser simulation that produced this (`AKS_Armstrong_Gate_Simulation.html`)
  was **not found** in Drive or the uploads; its assumptions could not be
  audited. Its number is tagged `unsupported` until the file is supplied.
- "The only architecture where harvesting improves the ride." Q3 answers
  this with sign and magnitude; the Sept 2026 deck already softens it to
  "harvesting smooths the ride without placing drag on the vehicle."
- "Generator side is trivial: 10 kW is only 16 N·m at 6,000 rpm. The binding
  constraint is road energy input." Correct, and it is the reason the road
  power invariance result matters so much.
- Sept 2026 pptx adds "THE COIL SPRING — 5% of the Kinetic Pool" (the damper
  is said to see ~5%). No derivation; `asserted`.

## 7. `AKS_Military_Brief_2026-10-11.pdf`

**Establishes — the best-sourced numbers in the AKS corpus.**
Abdelkareem et al. 2021: 30–334 W per damper off-road at 10–40 km/h on an
SUV, 3–25 W city, rebound dissipates ~6× compression (`measured`,
literature). Li/Zuo 2013 MMR: 15.4 W/corner at 15 mph pavement (`measured`,
literature). Formsprag rating basis 1e6 full-torque cycles with indexing
service factors 2–6; at 2 Hz that is ~139 h at catalog torque (`derived`).
Coil-sprung candidate fleets: HMMWV (~50,000 retained past 2050), M-ATV
(~6,350, TAK-4 coil, 16 in travel), ISV, ISV-Heavy, MRZR Alpha, Flyer 72;
not JLTV (gas), FMTV (leaf), Stryker (hydropneumatic). Expected AKS harvest
stated there as 60–300 W/corner rough ground, 0.25–1.2 kW per vehicle,
near zero on pavement (`derived`, conservative, consistent with this repo's
framing).

**Leaves open.** No HMMWV damper-power measurement exists anywhere; the
brief recommends an endurance-first test. Both feed `05_bench_protocol.md`
and the component-sizing report (Q5).

## 10. Prior repo state: `gate-twin` (commit f0db5c6, this branch)

**What exists and is carried forward (audited, not rewritten blindly).**

- Event-driven hybrid quarter-car + Gate ODE (`model.py`, now
  `vehicles/quarter_car_gate_legacy.py`): modes free/engA/engB, sprags as
  exact kinematic constraints, engaged-mode coupled mass matrix with
  reflected inertance `Jr = J·Gt²`, energy integrals as ODE states, audit

  `elements/gate.py` + `vehicles/quarter_car.py`.
- ISO 8608 synthesis (`roads/iso8608.py`), PSD-verified test.
- PM generator and claw-pole alternator models with exact `T·ω = P_elec +
  P_loss`.
- Provenance-enforcing parameter loader and `params.yaml`.
- V1 analytic gates (kinematic ratio, spin-down, zero/positive loop area,
  <1% closure).

**What it got wrong and is superseded here.**

- It carried the V2/V3/V4 "anchor" plan with Zuo MMR ≈40 W at 0.047 m/s
  RMS as a target. The 40.4 W figure is a *bench* result at a specific
  harmonic excitation on a resistive load, not a road RMS condition, and
  must not be used as a road-input anchor (see `04_prior_art.md`).
- Baseline parameters were F-150-class with several `[ASSUMPTION]` tags that
  must now be moved to `09_open_parameters.md` with ranges and swept.
- Transmission was fixed as a screw (lead 1 in/rev × step-up 6). The patent
  does not fix the mechanism; `r` is now a parameter.
- No linear-quarter-car analytic validation, no Zuo 100–400 W baseline
  reproduction, no ride/handling metrics, no φ / P_net definitions.

**`aks_quarter_car_v0.py`, `AKS_ClaudeCode_Prompt_DigitalTwin.md`,
`AKS_Armstrong_Gate_Simulation.html`, `Housing plate 1/2.pdf`, `larry drawing

## 12. What this intake changes about the brief's plan

1. Validation (b) in the brief — "with the Gate removed, reproduce Zuo's
   100–400 W" — is reachable analytically as well as numerically, because
   the quarter-car mean dissipated power under an ISO road has a closed
   form. We implement both and use the analytic form as the gate.
2. The road-power invariance result (same closed form) is the single most
   important physics fact for AKS's public story and is promoted to a
   first-class finding (`08_findings_that_change_the_story.md`, entry 1)
   before any experiment is run, because every experiment will exhibit it.
3. The 14 W per corner "early prototype data" the brief asks the README to
   state as `measured` was **not found in any supplied document**. The
   Diligence Response states the prototype has not been run; the Proof of
   Concept page says instrumentation is Aug–Oct 2026. Until a dated test
   record is supplied, the README says so and reports zero measured AKS
   numbers. This is deliberate.
